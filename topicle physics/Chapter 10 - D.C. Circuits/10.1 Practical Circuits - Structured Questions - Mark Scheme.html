Explain why the electromotive force (e.m.f) of a cell with internal resistance may be more than the terminal potential difference (p.d.) of the cell.

Mark Scheme (2 marks):

The e.m.f of a cell with internal resistance may be more than the terminal p.d. of the cell because:

  • When the cell drives a current; [1 mark]

  • Per unit charge; [1 mark]

Part (d) [3 marks]

The second 11 Ω resistor is now connected in parallel to the initial 11 Ω resistor in Fig 1.1. 

Qualitatively explain the effect this has on the battery. 

Mark Scheme (3 marks):

When the 11 Ω resistor is added in parallel to the initial 11 Ω resistor:

  • The battery heats up / there is a heating effect / gets hotter; [1 mark]

  • Because the (circuit) resistance decreases; [1 mark]

  • So the current / I{"language":"en","fontFamily":"Times New Roman","fontSize":"18","autoformat":true} increases; [1 mark]

[Total: 3 marks]

The current increase affects all components of the circuit, including the battery. Heating effects are always related to the current (which links to the power dissipated).

'Qualitatively' means no calculations are required in this question, however, it may help you to use numerical relationships to help you form your answer. Remember that when resistors are connected in parallel, their overall resistance is less than the resistance of each resistor. You can calculate the total resistance for these two resistors connected in parallel to prove this - you should get a value of 5.5 Ω.

Question 4 medium [13 marks]
Part (a) [3 marks]

Two circuits X and Y shown in Fig 1.1 were used by a student to test a battery of four identical cells. In circuit X the battery is connected to a voltmeter registering 12 V and there was no load resistor. In circuit Y a load resistor of 5.2 Ω is connected in parallel with an ammeter registering 0.41 A and a voltmeter registering 4.9 V. Assume that the meters in the circuits are ideal.

Fig. 1.1

Explain why there is a difference in voltages recorded in the two circuits.

Mark Scheme (3 marks):

Internal resistance:

  • The battery has internal resistance; [1 mark] 

Lost volts:

Any one from:

  • Some p.d. is used (or lost) to pass current (or charge) through the internal resistance; [1 mark]

  • The internal resistance results in / leads to lost volts; [1 mark]

  • Terminal p.d. V = e.m.f. – lost volts (= e.m.f. ­– (current × internal resistance)); [1 mark]

Difference in lost volts between circuits X and Y:

  • In X, there is no current / it is an open circuit (therefore) no p.d. is lost; [1 mark]

    OR

  • In Y, there is a current (therefore) p.d. is lost; [1 mark] 

[Total: 3 marks]

In circuit X, there is no closed loop, so no current flows. This is known as an open circuit, and the voltmeter reads the full e.m.f. since no voltage is lost across the internal resistance.
