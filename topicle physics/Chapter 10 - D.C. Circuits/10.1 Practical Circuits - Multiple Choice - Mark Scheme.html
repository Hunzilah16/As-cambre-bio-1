Question 1 easy [1 mark]

A battery is marked 9.0 V.

What does this mean ?

A

each coulomb of charge from the battery supplies 9.0 J of energy to the whole circuit
B

the battery supplies 9.0 J to an external circuit for each coulomb of charge
C

the potential difference across any component connected to the battery will be 9.0 V
D

there will always be 9.0 V across the battery terminals
Mark Scheme & Explanation:

The correct answer is A because:

  • A battery being marked 9.0 V means that each coulomb of charge from the battery supplies 9.0 J of energy to the whole circuit (this includes the internal resistance of the battery)

B is incorrect because this would mean that the amount of energy that the battery supplies to an external circuit for each coulomb of charge is not 9.0 V

C is incorrect because if there is no internal resistance in the battery (and the wires have negligible resistance), the sum of p.d. across the components connected to the battery will be 9.0 V

D is incorrect because the e.m.f of a battery does not tell anything about its internal resistance. If the battery has some internal resistance, the p.d. across the battery terminals will not be exactly 9.0 V, but less

Question 12 hard [1 mark]

In the circuit below, a voltmeter of resistance RV and an ammeter of resistance RA are used to measure the resistance R of the fixed resistor.

Which condition is necessary for an accurate value to be obtained for R ?

A

R is much smaller than RV
B

R is much smaller than RA
C

R is much greater than RV
D

R is much greater than RA
Mark Scheme & Explanation:

The correct answer is A because:

  • To obtain the resistance of resistor R from the set-up shown, the potential difference and current flowing through the resistor are measured

    • Ohm's law states V=IR{"language":"en","fontFamily":"Times New Roman","fontSize":"18","autoformat":true} , so resistance R=VI{"language":"en","fontFamily":"Times New Roman","fontSize":"18","autoformat":true}

  • In this circuit the voltmeter is connected in parallel with R (reading the true potential difference across it), while the ammeter sits in the main line and reads the total current — the current through R plus the current diverted through the voltmeter

  • Ideally, a voltmeter should have infinite resistance so that it draws negligible current; if its resistance is finite, some current bypasses the ammeter reading and the measured value of R is too low

  • For the diverted current to be negligible, the voltmeter resistance must be much larger than R, so that IV ≪ IR

  • Therefore, in order to obtain an accurate value of R, the necessary condition is that R should be much smaller than RV

B & D are incorrect because RA does not affect the result. The voltmeter reads the potential difference across R directly, and the ammeter sits in the main supply line, so its resistance RA only reduces the total current drawn from the supply — it does not change the ratio VI{"fontFamily":"Times New Roman","fontSize":"18","autoformat":true,"toolbar":""}, and so the measured value of R is unaffected by whether RA is small or large

C is incorrect because an ideal voltmeter has infinite resistance, so R could not be higher than this

Note that the ammeter is not in series with resistor R alone — it is in series with the parallel combination of the voltmeter and the resistor. So the ammeter measures IR + IV, not the current through R
