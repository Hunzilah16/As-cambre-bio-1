Part (a) [3 marks]

State three conditions which must apply to a pair of coplanar forces to make them a couple.

Mark Scheme (3 marks):

Three conditions which must apply to a pair of coplanar forces to make them a couple:

  • Equal in magnitude; [1 mark]

  • Opposite in direction; [1 mark]

  • Perpendicular to the distance between them OR parallel and in opposite directions; [1 mark]

Question 3 medium [10 marks]
Part (a) [4 marks]

The torque of a couple is given by:

torque = F × s

With the aid of a diagram explain what is meant by a couple. Label F  and s  on your diagram. State the appropriate unit for the torque of a couple.

Mark Scheme (4 marks):

A couple is defined as:

  • A pair of forces with equal magnitude AND opposite direction; [1 mark]

  • They do not pass through the same point OR they are separated by a distance; [1 mark]

A diagram illustrating F  and s  may look like:

  • Two forces with equal magnitude (both labelled F) and opposite direction; [1 mark]

  • s labelled as the perpendicular distance between the forces; [1 mark]

[Total: 4 marks]

The circle in the diagram is not necessary for the marks, but couples tend to act on objects and this circle represents the object.

Part (b) [2 marks]

The see-saw shown in Fig 1.1 consists of a uniform beam freely pivoted at the centre of the beam. Two children sit opposite each other so that the see-saw is in equilibrium.

Fig 1.1

Explain why the see-saw is in equilibrium.

Mark Scheme (2 marks):

The see-saw is in equilibrium because:

  • In equilibrium, anticlockwise moments = clockwise moments; [1 mark]

  • Moment due to weight of child on the left = moments due to weight of child on the right; [1 mark] 

[Total: 2 marks]

Part (c) [2 marks]

Explain why the weight of the beam does not affect equilibrium.

Mark Scheme (2 marks):

The beam's weight does not affect equilibrium because:

  • The weight of beam acts at the centre; [1 mark]

  • This is the location of the pivot; [1 mark]

OR

  • Centre of mass (point where weight of the beam acts) is at the pivot; [1 mark]

  • Moment = Force × Distance; at the pivot distance = 0, so moment = 0 (N m) ; [1 mark] 

[Total: 2 marks]

Part (d) [2 marks]

The beam is removed from the see-saw and placed on its end, as in Fig 1.2.

Fig 1.2

Explain why the beam is much more able to topple in this position, compared to when it is laying flat on the ground.

Mark Scheme (2 marks):

The beam is less stable because:

  • The base is narrow; [1 mark]

  • The line of action of the beam's centre of gravity can easily move out of the base; [1 mark]

[Total: 2 marks]

Question 4 medium [13 marks]
Part (a) [2 marks]

In our galaxy, some star systems are binary. This means the system has two stars.

One example is the binary system with stars Sirius A and Sirius B. This is shown in Fig 1.1.

Fig 1.1

Compare the different positions of Sirius B's centre of gravity and its centre of mass.

Explain your answer.

Mark Scheme (2 marks):

To compare the centre of gravity and centre of mass:

  • The centre of mass (of Sirius B) is located in the centre of the sphere / circle;

  • The centre of gravity is located closer to Sirius A;

[Total: 2 marks]

The centre of mass is the point which behaves the same as the object, if the object's mass were concentrated to that single point.

The centre of gravity is the point towards which the weight of a small object appears to act. As Sirius A is so large, this point is somewhere between Sirius A's and Sirius B's centre of mass. 

Part (b) [4 marks]

On a planet in this system, a young alien is playing with some uniform blocks on a sloped surface, shown in Fig 1.2.

Fig 1.2

Assuming friction is negligible, determine whether the block will slide or topple. 

Explain your answer.

Mark Scheme (4 marks):

The block's motion is:

  • The block will slide; [1 mark]

This is because:

  • The block's centre of gravity is at the geometric centre / midway along the base and midway up the block's height; [1 mark]

  • This lies above the block's base; [1 mark]

  • (However,) the block will slide as there is no / negligible friction; [1 mark]

[Total: 4 marks]

If, by eye, you are unsure if the centre of gravity lies above the base, annotate the diagram to confirm. We know the centre of gravity will be at the shape's geometric centre, as it is uniform. Drawing a line straight down from this will then confirm whether the shape will topple or not. Here, the diagram clearly shows the block will not topple, but slide.

The person applying the force to the spanner changes their grip to obtain a bigger torque. The magnitude of the force applied remains the same.

State and give a reason for the angle the force should be applied to the spanner to obtain its maximum possible value. 

Mark Scheme (3 marks):

The angle the force should be applied to the spanner is:

  • At right angles / perpendicular / 90°; [1 mark]

  • The torque is calculated using the perpendicular component of the force applied; [1 mark]

Question 7 hard [17 marks]
Part (a) [6 marks]

In the 1st century CE Greek-Egyptian engineer Hero of Alexandria described a simple steam engine, which was at least 100 years old when he wrote about it.

Now commonly called a Hero's engine, the machine is a steam turbine which can be made to rotate by boiling water in a sealed vessel beneath it. Two pipes channel the steam produced through nozzles which project outwards.

Fig. 1.1. shows Hero's engine in operation. 

Fig. 1.1. 

Explain why the vessel rotates around its axis

i) by referring to energy stores and transfers.

[3]

ii) by referring to forces, naming any relevant laws.

[3]

Mark Scheme (6 marks):

Explain why the vessel rotates around its axis:

i) by referring to energy stores and transfers:

Any three from:

  • Chemical energy store converted to thermal energy store as the fire burns; [1 mark]

  • The water boils (undergoing) vaporisation / turns to steam; [1 mark]

  • Nozzles create jets of steam which are perpendicular to the line between them; [1 mark]

  • Thermal energy store transfers to kinetic energy; [1 mark]

ii) by referring to forces, naming any relevant laws:

Any three from:

  • The vessel exerts a force on the steam to eject it; [1 mark]

  • According to Newton's third law, the steam exerts an equal and opposite reaction force on the nozzle / vessel; [1 mark]

  • There is an unbalanced force on the vessel; [1 mark]

  • According to Newton's second law / F = ma, the vessel accelerates; [1 mark]

  • A force applied at a distance from the pivot produces torque; [1 mark]

  • Two forces applied at equal distances from the pivot produce a couple; [1 mark]

  • Torque (of a couple) = one of the forces × distance between the nozzles / force; [1 mark]

[Total: 6 marks]
